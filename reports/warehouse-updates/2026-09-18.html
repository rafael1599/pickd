PickD — What's new

Friday, September 18, 2026 · for everyone on the Ludlow floor

The short version

1. A cancelled order's bikes wait on the CANCELLED PALLET, in the shipping area — and PickD picks from there before any shelf.

2. RETURN TO STOCK now means the opposite: where bikes rest, picked only when nowhere else has the SKU.

3. An order combined after being completed no longer takes its bikes off the shelf twice.

What you will notice

1. Cancelled orders wait on the CANCELLED PALLET now, and it is picked first.

When an order is cancelled after being completed, its bikes are not on their shelves any more — they are on a pallet. Until yesterday PickD wrote them to RETURN TO STOCK; from today they go to the CANCELLED PALLET, in the shipping area, on the route right after ROW 43.

And PickD takes them from there before it sends anybody to a shelf. A bike on that pallet already came off a row and owes somebody a put-away trip: the next order that needs it is that trip. Sending a picker to ROW 42 instead leaves it where it is, and the pile grows.

RETURN TO STOCK now means the opposite: where bikes rest. PickD only sources from it when no other location has the SKU — even though it sits early on the route, before ROW 10.

On the floor: a picking card that says CANCELLED PALLET means the bike is on that pallet, not on a row.

2. An order combined after being completed no longer takes its bikes off the shelf twice.

Combining a finished order with an open one reopens the finished one — that is how the Add-On flow works. When the pair was completed together, PickD deducted the reopened order a second time: it had already taken those bikes off the shelf when it was first completed.

What it cost: nothing looked wrong on screen. The stock just went down twice, so the shelf held bikes PickD thought were gone — seven orders, 35 units, three of them this week (#881373, #881488, #881612).

The four units from this week are back on their shelves.

3. A combined order left in the middle of verification no longer comes apart on its own.

While one half of a combined order is being verified, the other half waits in a reopened state. If that verification took more than two hours — a big order, a split shift, lunch — PickD closed the reopened half by itself and the combination was undone, with nobody told. Now it waits as long as the other half is still open.

4. And cancelling tells you when it did nothing.

If an order of the pallet is already marked as shipped, cancelling writes nothing — that part was already true. What was missing is that it said so; it used to look like a cancel that worked.

Now: "Nothing was cancelled: an order of this pallet is marked as shipped (#881504)" — naming the one that went on a truck.
