PickD — What's new

Friday, September 18, 2026 · for everyone on the Ludlow floor

The short version

1. A cancelled order's bikes wait on the CANCELLED PALLET, in the shipping area — and PickD picks from there before any shelf.

2. RETURN TO STOCK now means the opposite: where bikes rest, picked only when nowhere else has the SKU.

3. An order combined after being completed no longer takes its bikes off the shelf twice.

4. Inbound containers (like 7005N) are never suggested as pick locations.

5. Combined orders show their true pallet count across all boards without inflating totals.

6. Expired logins prompt you to sign back in instead of failing silently with an "Unknown" profile (a probable cause for intermittent label printing issues, with diagnostics added).

What you will notice

1. Cancelled orders wait on the CANCELLED PALLET now, and it is picked first.

When an order is cancelled after being completed, its bikes are not on their shelves any more — they are on a pallet. Until yesterday PickD wrote them to RETURN TO STOCK; from today they go to the CANCELLED PALLET, in the shipping area, on the route right after ROW 43.

And PickD takes them from there before it sends anybody to a shelf. A bike on that pallet already came off a row and owes somebody a put-away trip: the next order that needs it is that trip. Sending a picker to ROW 42 instead leaves it where it is, and the pile grows.

RETURN TO STOCK now means the opposite: where bikes rest. PickD only sources from it when no other location has the SKU — even though it sits early on the route, before ROW 10.

On the floor: a picking card that says CANCELLED PALLET means the bike is on that pallet, not on a row.

2. An order combined after being completed no longer takes its bikes off the shelf twice.

Combining a finished order with an open one reopens the finished one — that is how the Add-On flow works. When the pair was completed together, PickD deducted the reopened order a second time: it had already taken those bikes off the shelf when it was first completed.

What it cost: nothing looked wrong on screen. The stock just went down twice, so the shelf held bikes PickD thought were gone — seven orders, 35 units, three of them this week (#881373, #881488, #881612).

The four units from this week are back on their shelves.

3. A combined order left in the middle of verification no longer comes apart on its own.

While one half of a combined order is being verified, the other half waits in a reopened state. If that verification took more than two hours — a big order, a split shift, lunch — PickD closed the reopened half by itself and the combination was undone, with nobody told. Now it waits as long as the other half is still open.

4. And cancelling tells you when it did nothing.

If an order of the pallet is already marked as shipped, cancelling writes nothing — that part was already true. What was missing is that it said so; it used to look like a cancel that worked.

Now: "Nothing was cancelled: an order of this pallet is marked as shipped (#881504)" — naming the one that went on a truck.

5. Receiving containers are never suggested for picking.

When stock was checked into a container at the dock (such as 7005N), Double Check could sometimes suggest that container number as a place to pick bikes from. Containers are staging vessels on the receiving dock, not warehouse pick slots.

PickD now excludes container numbers from all picking paths, DCV suggestions, and stock lookups. Pickers are only directed to real shelf rows and floor locations.

Now: container codes never appear as pick locations on picking cards or in Double Check.

6. Combined orders no longer inflate pallet counts or board numbers.

Combining two orders onto the same pallet was showing two pallets where only one physical pallet was being built. The Live Board counters (Priority, FedEx, Regular, Waiting, and Pulling) were also counting combined orders as separate individual orders, showing inflated numbers on the wall.

Now, combining orders calculates pallet requirements across the entire shared cart. If an order shares a pallet, PickD counts it as one pallet across both orders, matching what is physically built on the floor.

Before: two combined small orders showed 2 pallets on the board instead of 1 shared pallet.

7. Carrier selection syncs across all sister orders in a combination.

When multiple orders share a pallet, setting the carrier on one of them in Ship or Live Board used to leave the other sister orders unchanged. Someone had to find each sister order and set the same carrier again by hand.

Now, choosing a carrier on any part of a combined order automatically syncs that carrier to all other sister orders on the same pallet.

8. Carrier and Load # / BOL now open in a single step.

Assigning freight previously took multiple separate clicks: selecting the carrier, confirming, and then finding a separate prompt to type in the Load Number or BOL.

Selecting a freight carrier now brings up the Load # / BOL field right there in the same dialog, so both can be entered and confirmed in a single move in both Live Board and Ship.

Now: select carrier → type Load # / BOL → confirm once.

9. Double Check picks multiple sublocations from the highest letter down to A.

When an order needs items from multiple sublocations along the same row, Double Check previously routed pickers starting from bin A and working up (A, B, C, D). Floor operations work much smoother by picking from the higher bins down.

DCV now guides the pick from the highest letter down to A (for example, D first, then C, B, and finally A).

10. Cleaner screens: two informational banners retired.

Two banners that added visual clutter on phones and tablets have been removed: the "Picking from: [locations]" header at the top of DCV, and the "Live sync lost. Falling back to slow polling" banner that popped up during momentary network hiccups.

The systems behind them remain fully active — location sorting and automatic background network polling both continue uninterrupted — but without taking up screen space.

11. "Done Editing" stays out of the way while editing a SKU.

When adjusting an order — replacing a bike, adjusting quantity, or removing a line — opening the action panel would cause the bottom "Done Editing" button to disappear and jump around, making it easy to tap the wrong button by mistake.

The editing drawer now holds its layout steady. The bottom "Done Editing" button cleanly steps aside only while you are actively confirming an individual item adjustment, and reappears as soon as you finish.

12. Order progress bars now animate while work is in progress.

Progress bars on order cards across the board now show an active moving indicator whenever an order has work underway. When the order reaches 100% verified, the bar settles solid, making it obvious from across the floor which orders are actively moving.

13. Ship > Waiting keeps the entire combined order visible.

If one order in a combined pallet was flagged as waiting for inventory, its sister orders could previously drop out of the Waiting tab in Ship, making it look like only part of the order existed.

Now, if any order in a combination is waiting on inventory, the entire combined group appears together in Ship > Waiting so the full pallet stays accounted for.

14. Shipping addresses come from the order paperwork, not the general account.

In Ship, the destination address was defaulting to the customer's general corporate account address instead of the specific store on the order document. For retail chains with multiple locations (like JAX) or consumer orders, this showed the wrong delivery location on screen.

Ship now pulls the exact ship-to address from the order's own paperwork. In addition, any manual address corrections made in the shipping form now save directly to that order and persist permanently.

Now: Ship displays the exact delivery address from the packing document, and manual corrections stay saved.

15. Expired logins no longer fail silently with an "Unknown" profile.

When an authentication session timed out on a handheld device, the user profile in the corner would sometimes turn to "Unknown". Taking a verification photo, completing an order, or printing labels would then fail with vague errors, without telling you that you needed to sign back in.

The app now recognizes expired sessions immediately and prompts for a clean sign-in instead of failing in the background. It also refreshes your user profile automatically whenever you unlock your device or switch back to the tab.

If label printing gives an error, the screen now shows the real cause instead of a generic message — that detail is what's needed to confirm whether it's the same expired-session issue the next time it happens.
